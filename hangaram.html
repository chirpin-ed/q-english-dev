지문 · 2023년 고2 11월 학평 32번

Information encountered after an event can influence subsequent remembering. External information can easily integrate into a witness’s memory, especially if the event was poorly encoded or the memory is from a distant event, in which case time and forgetting have degraded the original memory. With reduced information available in memory with which to confirm the validity of post-event misinformation, it is less likely that this new information will be rejected. Instead, especially when it fits the witness’s current thinking and can be used to create a story that makes sense to him or her, it may be integrated as part of the original experience. This process can be explicit, but it is often unconscious. That is, the witness might find himself or herself thinking about the event differently without awareness. Over time, the witness may not even know the source of information that led to the (new) memory.

다섯 줄 요약 · 대비

요약문의 빈칸에 알맞은 말을 <보기>에서 골라 쓰시오.

One side of the witness’s mind (1) new information against what is already stored. The other side takes that information in and (2) it into the remembered event. Which side wins depends on how much the original memory still holds, since a (3) trace leaves little to check against. So the checking side weakens as time and (4) do their work, and the absorbing side grows stronger. The witness experiences the result as a single memory, (5) that the two sides ever met.

주어진 글 뒤에 이어질 글의 순서로 가장 적절한 것은? (수특 C09-U01 초변형)

Recognizing a printed word is neither purely visual nor purely linguistic. Skilled reading depends on a rapid interaction between what the eyes take in and what previous experience has made available to the brain.

(A) In the case of familiar words, these established connections allow identification to occur so quickly that recognition and meaning are registered simultaneously. Any words not read before take longer because they must be worked out from pronounceable spelling patterns or decoded letter by letter. With enough repeated encounters, however, such words can eventually be stored as sight words and no longer require fresh decoding.

(B) During a single visual fixation, a skilled reader can perceive nearly all of the relevant letters or letter-groups in a word. The brain does not then treat every letter as a completely new item; it can immediately recognize familiar groups and recurring patterns within the string.

(C) These letter patterns have acquired their usefulness through earlier reading experience. Repeated encounters associate them with pronounceable sound units of known words, and the resulting visual-sound connections become stored in long-term memory.

① (A)–(C)–(B)   ② (B)–(A)–(C)   ③ (B)–(C)–(A)   ④ (C)–(A)–(B)   ⑤ (C)–(B)–(A)

③. (B) 눈으로 본 글자 → 패턴을 알아봄. (C) These letter patterns 가 (B)의 패턴을 받아 기억에 저장. (A) these established connections 가 (C)의 연결을 받아 익숙한 단어와 낯선 단어로 나뉨.
